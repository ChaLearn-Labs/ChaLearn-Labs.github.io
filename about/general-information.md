Machine learning is the science of building hardware or software that can learn to perform tasks from examples, usually pairs of inputs and outputs. Once trained, a machine can make predictions on new, unseen inputs. Applications range from automatic reading of handwriting and assisted medical diagnosis, to automatic text classification and spam filtering, to financial prediction.

**ChaLearn** organizes challenges and benchmarks to stimulate research in machine learning. We bring together researchers, students, and practitioners around well-defined problems with real datasets, clear evaluation metrics, and open leaderboards. The websites of past challenges stay open after the competition ends, so they keep serving as long-running benchmarks for the community.

Our current work spans several areas:

- **Looking at People (LAP)** — a challenge series pushing the state of the art in computer vision for detecting, recognizing, and interacting with humans, including gesture recognition and face anti-spoofing.
- **Fair Universe** — a series of challenges on bias and systematic errors in High Energy Physics simulations, co-organized with Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory.
- **MediChal** — challenges on medical applications of machine learning.
- **L2RPN (Learning to Run a Power Network)** — a series on real-world control of electricity transmission grids, run with RTE, the French grid operator.
- **AutoML** — automatic machine learning, building systems that can choose their own models, architectures, and hyperparameters without human intervention.

We also actively support the development of **Codabench**, an open-source platform for hosting machine learning competitions, and maintain the **Challenges in Machine Learning (CiML)** book series documenting the methodology and results behind our competitions.
